# 📄 Product Requirements Document (PRD)

## Unified Identity, Roles & Multi-Store Architecture

**Product:** Manooch E-commerce Platform
**Document Type:** Architecture & Logic Correction PRD
**Scope:** Identity model, role model, store ownership, customer referencing, app separation
**Contains:** Problem definition, business rules, expected behavior
**Does NOT contain:** Code, database schema, implementation details

---

# 1. Executive Summary

Manooch is a multi-tenant e-commerce platform where sellers get their own storefront (with its own domain) and manage it through a separate admin application.

The platform currently suffers from **incorrect identity modeling**. "Customer" and "Seller" are treated as two different kinds of people, when in reality they are **the same person with different roles**.

In addition:

* Store identity is incorrectly bound to a phone number.
* Storefront and Admin responsibilities overlap through an "owner view" feature.
* Customer records risk being duplicated per store instead of referencing one central identity.

This PRD defines the problems, the correct conceptual model, and the business rules required to fix them.

---

# 2. Current Architecture (As-Is)

| Component | Domain | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Admin App | `admin.manooch.com` | Seller business management (separate Next.js app) |
| Storefront App | `storefront.manooch.com` | Public customer-facing store (separate Next.js app) |
| Central Database | Shared | Contains users, customers, sellers, stores |

**Current behavior:**

* Users register as **either** a customer **or** a seller.
* Sellers register through a different flow, which generates a store website for them.
* Customers cannot upgrade their account to start a business.
* Sellers cannot act as customers on other stores.
* One phone number cannot be attached to more than one store.
* When a store owner visits their own storefront while logged in, they see a special **"owner view"** version of the site.

---

# 3. Problem List (Sorted by Severity)

## 🔴 P0 — Critical Architectural Problems

### P0-1. Identity Model Is Incorrect (Customer and Seller Are Separate Identities)

The system models "Customer" and "Seller" as two different user types instead of one user with multiple roles.

**Consequences:**
* A customer cannot start a business or open a store.
* A seller cannot shop on another store as a customer.
* The same real person may need two accounts.
* Role logic is duplicated and inconsistent across apps.

**Real-world truth being violated:**
> A seller is also a customer of other stores. A customer can become a store owner at any time. They are the same person.

---

### P0-2. Store Identity Is Bound to Phone Number

The phone number currently acts as **both** the personal identity **and** the business identity.

```
Phone Number = User Identity
Phone Number = Store Identity
```

**Consequences:**
* One person can own only one store, permanently.
* A user cannot create a second store or brand.
* Personal contact data and business contact data cannot be separated.
* The platform cannot grow into a true marketplace.
* Users are forced to buy extra SIM cards / phone numbers to open another business.

**Open decision required:** Is this a deliberate business policy or a technical limitation? (See Section 9.)

---

### P0-3. Risk of Duplicate Customer Identities Across Stores

When a customer registers on a storefront, is imported by a seller, or is added manually by a seller, the system may create a **new user record per store** instead of referencing the existing central user.

**Consequences:**
* Same person exists multiple times in the database.
* Phone/email uniqueness conflicts.
* Broken authentication and login confusion.
* Inaccurate analytics and reporting.
* Impossible to build a unified customer profile.

---

## 🟠 P1 — Major Structural Problems

### P1-4. Owner View Inside Storefront (Responsibility Leak)

The storefront currently renders a special experience when the store owner is logged in.

**Consequences:**
* Admin logic leaks into a public customer-facing app.
* Permission checks are duplicated in two apps.
* Higher bug surface and unpredictable behavior.
* Violates the intended separation between Admin and Storefront.
* Confuses the owner about what real customers actually see.

**Decision:** This feature must be **completely removed**.

---

### P1-5. Application Responsibilities Overlap

Storefront sometimes behaves as a management surface; Admin sometimes assumes storefront context.

**Consequences:**
* Unclear ownership of features.
* Authorization logic spread across two apps.
* Harder to maintain, test, and secure.

---

### P1-6. No Business Creation / Upgrade Flow

There is no clean path such as:

> Logged-in user → "Start Selling" → Create Store → Get Admin Access

Seller registration is a separate, disconnected flow that produces a separate account.

**Consequences:**
* Customers cannot convert into sellers.
* Conversion funnel from buyer to merchant is broken.
* Growth potential is blocked.

---

## 🟡 P2 — Data Ownership & Governance Problems

### P2-7. Unclear Customer Data Ownership

It is not defined who owns customer identity: the platform or the store.

**Consequences:**
* Sellers may assume they own the customer account.
* Unclear rules for imports, exports, and deletion.
* Privacy and compliance risk.

---

### P2-8. Store-Level Customer Data Not Separated From Global Identity

Store-specific data (tags, notes, loyalty points, order history) is not clearly separated from the global user profile.

**Consequences:**
* One store's data may leak into another store's view.
* A seller could see a customer's activity on competitor stores.
* Serious privacy and trust violation in a multi-tenant platform.

---

# 4. Root Cause Analysis

All problems trace back to **three concepts being merged into one**:

| Concept | What It Actually Is | Currently Merged With |
|---|---|---|
| **Person (User)** | A human with login credentials | Store, Role |
| **Store (Business)** | A commercial entity | Phone number, User |
| **Role** | What a person can do in a context | User type at registration |

**Root cause statement:**
> The platform stores *who someone is*, *what they own*, and *what they can do* in a single flattened concept.

# 5. Goals & Non-Goals

## ✅ Goals

1. One global identity per real person.
2. A user can be a customer and a seller at the same time.
3. A user can shop on any store on the platform.
4. A user can create and own a store (or multiple stores — pending decision).
5. Stores reference existing users; they never create duplicate identities.
6. Store-specific data stays isolated per store.
7. Complete removal of owner view from storefront.
8. Strict separation between Admin app and Storefront app.
9. A clean "Start Selling" upgrade flow.

## ❌ Non-Goals (Out of Scope for This PRD)

* Payment provider changes
* Storefront UI/theme redesign
* Marketing site changes
* Seller subscription/billing model
* Multi-language or localization work

# 6. Target Conceptual Model (To-Be)

## 6.1 Three Separate Concepts

### 1️⃣ User (Person) — Platform Level

The single source of truth for identity.

Holds:
* Unique identifier (phone number)
* Email, name
* Authentication credentials
* Account status

Rules:
* Exists **once** in the entire platform.
* Can log in to Storefront (any store) and Admin.
* Is never duplicated per store.

---

### 2️⃣ Store (Business Entity) — Business Level

An independent commercial entity.

Holds:
* Store name, brand, domain
* **Its own business contact information** (business phone, business email, address)
* Legal/tax information
* Owner reference

Rules:
* A store is **not** identified by the owner's personal phone number.
* A store has its own business identity, separate from the owner's personal identity.

---

### 3️⃣ Role / Membership — Contextual Level

Defines what a user can do, and **where**.

Examples:
* Customer (global, implicit for every user)
* Store Owner (scoped to a specific store)
* Store Staff (scoped to a specific store) — future
* Platform Admin (global)

Rules:
* Roles are **contextual**, not registration types.
* A user can hold multiple roles simultaneously.
* Roles are granted after registration, not chosen at registration.

---

## 6.2 Relationship Model (Conceptual)

```
        ┌──────────────┐
        │     USER     │  (one global identity)
        └──────┬───────┘
               │
     ┌─────────┼────────────┐
     │                      │
 owns/manages          shops at
     │                      │
     ▼                      ▼
┌──────────┐        ┌──────────────────────┐
│  STORE   │◄───────│  STORE–CUSTOMER LINK │
└──────────┘        └──────────────────────┘
                     (relationship, not a
                      separate identity)
```

---

## 6.3 Store Customer = Relationship, Not Identity

A "store customer" is **only a link** between a Store and an existing User.

It may contain:
* Store-specific tags and segments
* Loyalty points for that store
* Internal notes written by that seller
* Order history within that store
* Date of first interaction with that store

It must **never** contain:
* A separate login
* A separate password
* A duplicated personal identity

# 7. Business Rules

## 7.1 Identity Rules

| ID | Rule |
|---|---|
| IR-1 | Every person has exactly one global user account. |
| IR-2 | Phone number is globally unique across the entire platform. |
| IR-3 | A user is never created twice, regardless of how many stores they interact with. |
| IR-4 | Authentication happens at platform level, not store level. |
| IR-5 | Registration does not ask "are you a customer or a seller" — everyone registers as a user. |

## 7.2 Role Rules

| ID | Rule |
|---|---|
| RR-1 | Every registered user is a customer by default. |
| RR-2 | Seller is an additional role, not a replacement for customer. |
| RR-3 | A user can hold customer and seller roles at the same time. |
| RR-4 | Store Owner role is always scoped to a specific store. |
| RR-5 | Roles are granted after account creation, never at registration. |

## 7.3 Store Rules

| ID | Rule |
|---|---|
| SR-1 | A store is a business entity with its own contact details. |
| SR-2 | A store's business phone is separate from the owner's personal phone. |
| SR-3 | A store must have exactly one owner (staff roles are future scope). |
| SR-4 | Store creation is an action performed by an existing logged-in user. |
| SR-5 | Multi-store ownership policy — **pending decision (Section 9).** |

## 7.4 Store–Customer Relationship Rules

| ID | Rule |
|---|---|
| CR-1 | A store never creates a user identity; it only creates a relationship. |
| CR-2 | Before creating any customer record, the system must check if the user already exists globally. |
| CR-3 | If the user exists → link to the store. If not → create the global user once, then link. |
| CR-4 | A seller can only see the customer's data related to their own store. |
| CR-5 | A seller cannot see a customer's activity, orders, or profile from other stores. |
| CR-6 | The platform owns identity. The store owns the relationship and its own transaction history. |

## 7.5 Application Separation Rules

| ID | Rule |
|---|---|
| AR-1 | Storefront is **always** a public, customer-facing experience — for everyone, including the store owner. |
| AR-2 | Storefront contains no management, editing, or owner-only functionality. |
| AR-3 | All store management happens exclusively in the Admin app. |
| AR-4 | Owner view in storefront is removed entirely, with no conditional variants. |
| AR-5 | If an owner wants to preview their store, they see exactly what a customer sees. |

# 8. Expected User Flows (Described, Not Implemented)

## Flow A — New Customer Registers on a Storefront

1. Visitor registers on `store-a.com`.
2. System checks the central identity database by phone number.
3. If the person does not exist → create one global user.
4. If the person already exists → do **not** create a new user.
5. Create a relationship linking that user to Store A.
6. User is now a customer of Store A only.

---

## Flow B — Existing Customer Shops on a Second Store

1. User already exists globally (registered previously via Store A).
2. User visits Store B and logs in / registers.
3. System recognizes the existing global identity.
4. A new relationship is created for Store B.
5. Store B sees them as a new customer of Store B.
6. Store B has **no visibility** into Store A activity.
7. Store A has **no visibility** into Store B activity.

---

## Flow C — Seller Imports or Manually Adds a Customer

1. Seller uploads a list or adds a customer manually in Admin.
2. For each entry, the system checks the central identity database.
3. If the person exists → create a store relationship only.
4. If the person does not exist → create one global user, then create the relationship.
5. Imported customers are never duplicated.
6. Imported customers belong to the platform identity, linked to that store.

---

## Flow D — Customer Becomes a Seller ("Start Selling")

1. Logged-in user clicks "Start Selling".
2. User fills in **business information** (business name, business contact, category, etc.).
3. Store entity is created and linked to the user as Owner.
4. User receives the Store Owner role for that store.
5. User gains access to the Admin app.
6. **The user keeps their customer role and shopping history unchanged.**

---

## Flow E — Seller Shops on Another Store

1. Seller logs in on any storefront using the same account.
2. They are treated as a normal customer of that store.
3. Their seller status is completely irrelevant and invisible there.
4. No owner tools, no admin links, no special UI.

---

## Flow F — Owner Visits Their Own Storefront

1. Owner opens their own storefront and logs in.
2. They see the **exact same experience as a normal customer**.
3. No owner view, no edit buttons, no admin banner, no special mode.
4. To manage anything, they must go to the Admin app.

# 9. Open Decisions (Must Be Resolved Before Build)

### 🔸 Decision 1 — Multi-Store Ownership Policy

Choose one model:

| | **Model A — Single Business** | **Model B — Multi-Store Marketplace** |
|---|---|---|
| Rule | One user = one store | One user = many stores |
| Store identity | Tied to user | Independent business entity |
| Pros | Simple, prevents spam sellers, easy control | Scalable, supports multi-brand owners and agencies |
| Cons | Blocks growth, forces duplicate accounts | More complex permissions and store switching |
| Best for | Controlled small marketplace | Long-term platform growth |

**Recommendation:** Model B — with a configurable limit on the number of stores per user (e.g. default 1, expandable on approval). This keeps control today without blocking growth tomorrow.

---

### 🔸 Decision 2 — Store Contact Identifier

* Should a store require its own business phone number, separate from the owner's personal phone?
* Must the store's business phone also be globally unique?

**Recommendation:** Yes to both — store contact must be a store attribute, not a user attribute.

---

### 🔸 Decision 3 — Customer Data Ownership Policy

* Can a seller export customers they imported themselves?
* Can a seller export customers who registered organically on their storefront?
* What happens to the relationship when a store is closed or suspended?

**Recommendation:** Sellers own the *relationship data*; the platform owns the *identity*. Export limited to store-scoped data only.

---

### 🔸 Decision 4 — Seller Approval

* Can any user instantly create a store?
* Or does store creation require manual review/approval?

---

### 🔸 Decision 5 — Existing Data Migration

* How do we handle current duplicate accounts of the same person (one as customer, one as seller)?
* Which record becomes the primary identity?
* How are orders and history merged?

---

# 10. Acceptance Criteria

The fix is considered complete when:

1. ✅ There is no separate "seller registration" — only user registration.
2. ✅ Any logged-in user can start a store from their account.
3. ✅ A seller can shop on any storefront as a normal customer with the same account.
4. ✅ The same phone number never produces two user records.
5. ✅ Registering on a second store creates a relationship, not a new identity.
6. ✅ Importing an existing customer links them instead of duplicating them.
7. ✅ A seller cannot view a customer's data belonging to another store.
8. ✅ Owner view no longer exists anywhere in the storefront app.
9. ✅ Storefront contains zero management functionality.
10. ✅ Admin app contains all management functionality.
11. ✅ Store business contact information is stored on the store, not the user.
12. ✅ Multi-store policy (Decision 1) is implemented consistently across both apps.

# 11. Risks

| Risk | Impact | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Existing duplicate accounts | High | Requires a merge/migration strategy |
| Cross-store data leakage during refactor | Critical | Privacy violation; needs strict scoping tests |
| Sellers expecting to "own" customers | Medium | Requires clear policy communication |

# 12. Summary Statement

> **The platform currently confuses three separate concepts: the person, the business, and the permission.**
>
> A person must exist once, globally. A business must be an independent entity with its own identity. A permission must be contextual and grantable at any time.
>
> Once these three are separated, every listed problem — customer/seller conflict, phone-to-store binding, duplicate customers, and the storefront owner view — resolves naturally.
